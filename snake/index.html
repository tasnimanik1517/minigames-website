Snake

The classic arcade game — eat, grow, and try not to run into yourself.

How to Play

Guide your snake around the board using the arrow keys, WASD, swipes, or the on-screen arrows. Every piece of food you eat makes your snake one segment longer and speeds the game up a little, so it gets trickier the longer you survive. The round ends the moment you hit a wall or run into your own tail — so plan your turns a few moves ahead, especially once the board starts feeling crowded. Tap or click anywhere to start, and again to play another round once you crash — your best score is saved automatically.

Tips to Score Higher

Stay near the middle of the board rather than hugging the walls — it gives you more room to react when the snake speeds up. Try to move in loose loops instead of long straight lines, so you're not forced into a corner when a tight turn comes up. As your snake grows, start planning two or three moves ahead instead of reacting one square at a time, and never chase food that only opens up next to your own tail.

About Snake

Snake traces back to Blockade, a 1976 arcade game from Gremlin Industries where two players steered growing lines around a board. The genre's best-known version arrived in 1997, when Nokia engineer Taneli Armanto built Snake for the Nokia 6110 — it later shipped on the Nokia 3310 and introduced an entire generation to mobile gaming. This version keeps that same simple, one-tap idea alive in the browser, with a fresh look and no download required.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you play the Snake game?

Move your snake with the arrow keys, WASD, swipes, or the on-screen arrows so it eats the food that appears on the board. Each piece eaten adds a segment and a point, and the game ends when the snake hits a wall or crosses its own body.

Who invented the Snake game?

The concept dates back to Blockade, a 1976 arcade game. The version most people remember was created by Nokia engineer Taneli Armanto in 1997 for the Nokia 6110, which is why Snake is so strongly associated with early mobile phones.

Is Snake good for your brain?

It's a light way to exercise reaction time, spatial planning, and short-term focus — you're constantly judging angles and planning your next few moves. It's not a substitute for serious cognitive training, but it's a genuinely engaging quick mental workout.

How can I get a higher score in Snake?

Keep to the open middle of the board, turn in loose curves rather than sharp last-second corners, and think a few moves ahead once your snake gets long rather than only reacting to the next square.

Can I play Snake on my phone?

Yes — this version works on mobile browsers with swipe controls or the on-screen arrow buttons, so no app or download is needed.
